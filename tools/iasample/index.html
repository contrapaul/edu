Problem statement

Where the writing goesCriterion B carries 1,200 words, the largest allocation on the project, in the smallest page allocation. This is the one place in the report where sustained paragraphs belong.

The current situation

[Name] is a freelance maker who sells handmade goods online. They work at a 1.2m desk against the wall of a shared room for two to three hours most evenings, and photograph a batch of finished pieces roughly once a week. The desk carries three zones of activity: a cutting mat, a sewing machine, and a corner kept clear for photographs. There is no ceiling light above the desk, and the window to the left is dark by [time] in winter.

All of this work is lit by a single weighted base LED task lamp, fixed in one corner of the desk, with a ring light that stays in its box because setting it up takes longer than the photographs do.

The current problem

The existing lamp cannot be positioned above or across the work, which means [Name] cannot light a piece evenly for photography or see fine detail without their own hand shadowing the work. Frames 4 and 5 of the task analysis show the failure: the head will not reach over the piece without the base entering the camera frame, so the light arrives from one side and half the subject falls into shadow.

Moving the lamp is itself a problem. It takes two hands, which the persona does not have free, and the cable snags on tools as the base is dragged. The consequence in frame 8 is images that are inconsistent enough to cause returns when a buyer sees the real colour of a piece.

SourceWhat it showed
Primary personaImage quality drives sales, so lighting is a business requirement rather than a comfort one
Task analysisThe task fails at frames 4 to 6: the head cannot get above the work, and the colour is fixed and warm
Product analysisProducts 1, 2 and 4 move or reach but light poorly; product 3 lights well but takes too long to set up
Why this problemNo product tested combines a diffuse, colour adjustable source with a mount that can be repositioned one handed

Opportunities for redesign

1.Position a diffuse light source directly above the work, clear of the camera frame (frames 4 and 5)
2.Reposition and aim the light with one hand, without stopping the task (frames 3 and 7)
3.Free the desk surface the current base occupies, and give control of colour temperature (frames 6 and 8)

Idea 1: Balanced arm with a detachable head

Feasible means buildableThree different mechanisms rather than three trims of one idea: an arm that reaches, a rail that slides, and a panel that leaves altogether. Each has to be something the workshop can build, because criterion D requires a physical model of whichever one is selected.
Sketch: clamped arm reaching over the desk
Sketch: head lifted off its magnetic mount
ASpring balanced arm, clamps to the desk edge
BHead detaches from a magnetic dock
CDiffuser panel spreads light over the whole piece
DStandard tripod thread behind the head

Concept

A spring balanced parallel arm clamps to the back edge of the desk and carries the head anywhere above the work. The head itself lifts off a magnetic dock, so it can be held, set down beside a piece, or screwed onto the camera tripod, and dropped back onto the arm one handed. The arm solves reach and the detachable head solves portability, where products 2 and 4 each solved only one of the two.

An extruded rail clamps along the back edge of the desk. A carriage rides the rail, locks with a lever, and carries a telescoping stem and the light head. Rather than moving the whole lamp between the three work zones, the persona slides the head along the desk. Power is carried in the rail, which removes the snagging cable of frame 3 entirely.
