Ai / Criterion A, Inquiring and analysing

Show that they need this

Statement of inquiry

Designers develop new functions by making devices that sense, decide and act for the people who need them.

Key concept
Development
Related concepts
Function, Innovation
Global context
Scientific and Technical Innovation
Time
2 classes
Worked
On your own
  1. 1Choose your client, then research with AI
  2. 2Laptops closed, write it by hand
Do2 classes
  • What the situation is now, with at least one piece of evidence you actually collected. Not something you assumed.
  • Why this has to be electronic. What will your device sense, work out, or remember that a person, a sign, or a phone your client already owns cannot do as well?
  • What happens if nobody makes anything. If the honest answer is "not much", you have not found the need yet.
Hand inEnd of class 2, handed in on paper
  • Need Statement, handwritten in class
  • Your research notes from class 1, however you made them.

Where it goes

A photo of your handwritten page to ManageBac, and the paper itself at the end of class 2

GradedAi

“Explains and justifies the need for a solution to a problem for a client/target audience.”

What separates the bands

1-2An incomplete need statement, with no evidence and no justification.
3-4A need statement with limited justification and no evidence you collected yourself.
5-6A need statement with some justification and at least one piece of evidence you collected.
7-8A detailed need statement, fully justified, with your own evidence and a clear argument for why this problem needs electronics.

Tip

Use AI hard in class 1. Ask it to argue against your idea, not just for it.

Tip

Take notes you can write from. In class 2 they are the only thing on your desk.

Tip

Choose a group outside your own grade, then go and ask them. Do not guess what they need.

Tip

Justifying a need means proving it, not asserting it.

Do1 class
  • Why this order? Which activity had to happen first, and what it allows you to start.

GradedAii

“Constructs a detailed research plan, which identifies and prioritizes the primary and secondary research needed to develop a solution to the problem independently.”

What separates the bands

1-2A basic plan that is unlikely to help you develop a device.
3-4A plan that will help you develop parts of your device, in no particular order.
5-6An organised plan, ordered by priority, that will help you develop your device.
7-8A detailed and organised plan, prioritised with reasons, including the bench test, where every row lets you decide something.

Tip

Every row should let you decide something. If it does not, take it off the plan.

Tip

Spend the tokens on the bench test. It is the only thing that tells you what is physically possible.

Tip

Reading about a sensor is not the same as watching one fail on your desk.

Tip

Write down what you gave up, and what could go wrong because you skipped it.

Aiii / Criterion A, Inquiring and analysing

Take apart four products, without opening them

Statement of inquiry

Designers develop new functions by making devices that sense, decide and act for the people who need them.

Key concept
Development
Related concepts
Function, Innovation
Global context
Scientific and Technical Innovation
Time
3 classes
Worked
On your own
  1. 1One card together, then your first one alone
  2. 2Two more cards
  3. 3The badly designed one, then your summary
Do3 classes
  • What it is, who it is for, what it costs, and how it gets its power.
  • What it must be sensing, and your evidence.
  • What it must be deciding. Look for behaviour a direct wire from input to output could not explain.
  • What it does back, and which output does it.
  • Your block diagram: what goes in, what happens in the middle, what comes out, with real catalog parts named.
  • Power: mains, batteries or rechargeable, and what that choice forced the designer to do.
  • Controls and lights: every button and every indicator, and what a user must already know to use it.
  • One sentence on what you would copy from this product, and what you would leave behind.
Hand inEnd of class 3
  • 4 Black Box Cards and a summary
  • Bring the cards to class for the summary discussion.

Where it goes

Photos of your four cards and a PDF of the summary to **ManageBac

GradedAiii

“Analyses a range of existing products that inspire a solution to the problem in detail.”

What separates the bands

1-2An incomplete analysis, of products unlikely to guide your work.
3-4Basic analysis of four products, though not all of them will help guide your work.
5-6Partly detailed analysis of four products, most of which will help guide your work.
7-8Detailed analysis of four products that all guide your work, with a summary that turns them into design implications.

Tip

You may share one product with a classmate, no more.

Tip

Do not open anything. Work out what is inside from how it behaves.

Tip

Name real catalog parts in your block diagrams, not just "a sensor".

Tip

The summary is where the marks are. Four cards and no summary stays at 5-6.

Bii / Criterion B, Developing ideas

Come up with a range of ideas

Statement of inquiry

Designers develop new functions by making devices that sense, decide and act for the people who need them.

Key concept
Development
Related concepts
Function, Innovation
Global context
Scientific and Technical Innovation
Time
3 classes
Worked
On your own
  1. 1Draw cards, make ideas fast
  2. 2Develop and annotate your best six
  3. 3Breadboard tests and the swap
Do3 classes
  • Block diagram. What goes in, what happens in the middle, what comes out, with real catalog parts named.
  • Sketch of the object. Roughly to scale, in the place it will live, next to a hand or a desk for size.
  • Three frame storyboard. What the user does, what the device does back, and what state it is left in.
  • Your notes, explaining how this idea helps your client and pointing at your Bi rules by number, plus one risk.
Hand inEnd of class 3
  • 6 ideas + 2 breadboard tests
  • A photo or short video of each breadboard test, with a note on what it proved.

Where it goes

Photos of all six idea sheets to **ManageBac

GradedBii

“Develops a range of feasible design ideas, using an appropriate medium(s) and detailed annotation, which can be correctly interpreted by others.”

What separates the bands

1-2Fewer than six complete ideas, with messy drawings and few notes.
3-4Six ideas with drawings and some notes.
5-6Six ideas with clean block diagrams, sketches and notes.
7-8Six well developed ideas with clean diagrams, storyboards and dense notes, plus two breadboard tests that prove the risky parts work.

Tip

Write far more notes than you think you need. You are explaining the idea to someone who is not you.

Tip

Use a ruler and drawing tools. Neat drawings are easier to annotate.

Tip

Build only the risky part on the breadboard. Twenty minutes is enough to kill a bad idea.

Tip

Six genuinely different ideas, not one idea in six colours.

3-4A choice with basic reasons, and no argument against it.
5-6A choice justified against your specification, with some argument against it.
7-8A choice justified in full against your specification by number, with the strongest case against it, and what you carried over from the ideas you rejected.

Statement of inquiry

Key concept

Time
1 class
Worked
On your own
Do1 class
  • Your critical path, marked. The chain of tasks that decides your finish date.
  • Order deadlines. Anything you buy, print or send to a machine gets a class number by which it must be started.
  • A "finished when" on every row. If a task has no finish condition, nobody can follow it.
  • A backup plan on every critical path row.
Hand inEnd of class 1
  • Build Plan, both versions

Where it goes

A PDF of both versions, before and after the swap test, to **ManageBac

GradedCi

“Constructs a detailed and logical plan, which describes the efficient use of time and resources, sufficient for peers to be able to follow to create the solution.”

What separates the bands

1-2A plan too incomplete for anyone else to follow.
3-4A basic plan, with some tasks and rough timings.
5-6A logical plan with timings and resources, which a classmate could mostly follow.
7-8A detailed and logical plan with a marked critical path, order deadlines, a finished-when on every row and a backup on every critical row, which a classmate can follow without asking you anything.

Tip

Mark the critical path. It is the chain that decides your finish date.

Tip

Every row needs a finished-when. "Work on the case" is not a task.

Tip

Put an order deadline on anything you have to buy, print or send to a machine.

Tip

Do the swap test honestly. What your partner cannot follow is what you have to fix.

Ciii / Criterion C, Creating the solution

Follow your plan and finish it

Statement of inquiry

Designers develop new functions by making devices that sense, decide and act for the people who need them.

Key concept
Development
Related concepts
Function, Innovation
Global context
Scientific and Technical Innovation
Time
End of the build classes
Worked
On your own
DoEnd of the build classes
  • It does what your specification says, including the decision it makes on its own.
  • It runs on its own power, with no laptop connected, for as long as your specification claims.
  • It is in a case, or deliberately open and neat, with every board fixed down and nothing loose.
  • Every button and every light is labelled, in words your client can read.
  • There is a way to turn it on and off.
  • The wiring is tidy enough to repair, and connectors unplug instead of being cut.
  • A one page user sheet: what it does, how to start it, what each light or sound means, and what to do when it stops.
Hand inLast build class
  • The finished device
  • The device itself, demonstrated in class and working on its own power.

Where it goes

Photos of the finished device and a PDF of your user sheet to **ManageBac

GradedCiii

“Follows the plan to create the solution, which functions as intended and is presented appropriately.”

What separates the bands

1-2A device that does not follow the plan and does not work.
3-4A device that follows the plan loosely and works with several problems.
5-6A device that follows the plan and works with few problems.
7-8A device that follows the plan, does what your specification says including its own decision, runs on its own power, and is finished well enough to hand to your client.

Tip

Test it on its own power, with no laptop attached, before you call it finished.

Tip

Label every button and every light in words your client can read.

Tip

Connectors should unplug, not be cut. You will need to repair it.

Tip

Write the one page user sheet as if you will not be there to explain anything.

2 classes

Tip

Point at your evidence in every row. A judgement with no evidence is an opinion.

What difference did it make?

Key concept
Development
Related concepts
Function, Innovation
Global context
Scientific and Technical Innovation
Time
2 classes
Worked
On your own
Do2 classes
  • What would you stop doing if you had this?
  • What would you start doing?
  • Who else in this room would notice it, and would they mind?
  • What would annoy you about it by the third week?

Hand inEnd of class 2

Your report as a PDF and your 3 minute demo video to **ManageBac

GradedDiv

“Explains the impact of the product on the client/target audience.”

What separates the bands

1-2Little explanation of what difference the device made.
3-4A basic explanation of the difference it made, with little evidence.
5-6An explanation of the impact on your client, with evidence from real use.
7-8A detailed explanation of the impact on your client, evidenced by the demo video and their own words, returning to the need you wrote in Ai.

Tip

Film your client using it, in the place it would live, doing the real task.

Tip

Their words matter more than yours. Record what they say.

Tip

Quote your own Ai need statement. That is where the report closes.

Tip

You take the device home at the end, so the video is the only record that it was used.
