# G10 Electronics Design — Task Sheet Contents

Working contents for the sixteen student task sheets, one per MYP Design strand. Same shape as [assessments.md](assessments.md) for Grade 9, and the sheets are generated from this file into [g10-task-sheets.html](../g10-task-sheets.html).

Layout is **Three Acts**: a masthead carrying the MYP framing, then three columns headed **Do**, **Hand in** and **Graded**, then a band of four tips along the foot.

| Source | What it supplied |

| [g10-electronics-design.html](../g10-electronics-design.html) | Task titles, time, hand-in artefacts, descriptors, requirement lists and class shapes. The G10 page is already written as a student guide in the second person, so most of it transfers without rewriting. |

## 1. Ai — Show that they need this

*Criterion A, Inquiring and analysing*

| Field | Value | |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 2 classes | FROM PAGE |
| Worked | On your own | FROM PAGE |
| Hand in | Need Statement, handwritten in class | FROM PAGE |
| Also submit | Your research notes from class 1, however you made them. | EDITORIAL |
| Deadline wording | End of class 2, handed in on paper | EDITORIAL |

### Do

Required on the submission:

- **What the situation is now**, with at least one piece of evidence you actually collected. Not something you assumed.
- **Why this has to be electronic.** What will your device sense, work out, or remember that a person, a sign, or a phone your client already owns cannot do as well?
- **What happens if nobody makes anything.** If the honest answer is "not much", you have not found the need yet.

Class shape, for the strip under the masthead:

1. Choose your client, then research with AI
2. Laptops closed, write it by hand

### Hand in

- Need Statement, handwritten in class
- Submit to **A photo of your handwritten page to ManageBac, and the paper itself at the end of class 2**
- Also submit: Your research notes from class 1, however you made them.

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Explains and justifies the need for a solution to a problem for a client/target audience.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | An incomplete need statement, with no evidence and no justification. | EDITORIAL |
| 3-4 | A need statement with limited justification and no evidence you collected yourself. | EDITORIAL |
| 5-6 | A need statement with some justification and at least one piece of evidence you collected. | EDITORIAL |
| 7-8 | A detailed need statement, fully justified, with your own evidence and a clear argument for why this problem needs electronics. | EDITORIAL |

### Tips

1. Use AI hard in class 1. Ask it to argue against your idea, not just for it.
2. Take notes you can write from. In class 2 they are the only thing on your desk.
3. Choose a group outside your own grade, then go and ask them. Do not guess what they need.
4. Justifying a need means proving it, not asserting it.

## 2. Aii — Plan your research

*Criterion A, Inquiring and analysing*

| Field | Value | |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 1 class | FROM PAGE |
| Worked | On your own | FROM PAGE |
| Hand in | Research Plan, 1 page | FROM PAGE |
| Also submit |  | TO WRITE |
| Deadline wording | End of class 1 | EDITORIAL |

### Do

Required on the submission:

- Why this order? Which activity had to happen first, and what it allows you to start.
- What did you give up? Name what you could not afford, and say what could go wrong because you skipped it.
- A bench test, budgeted at 3 tokens. It is the only activity that tells you whether your idea is physically possible.
- A "what this lets me decide" column on every row.

### Hand in

- Research Plan, 1 page
- Submit to A PDF of your research plan to **ManageBac**
- Also submit: *TO WRITE*

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Constructs a detailed research plan, which identifies and prioritizes the primary and secondary research needed to develop a solution to the problem independently.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | A basic plan that is unlikely to help you develop a device. | EDITORIAL |
| 3-4 | A plan that will help you develop parts of your device, in no particular order. | EDITORIAL |
| 5-6 | An organised plan, ordered by priority, that will help you develop your device. | EDITORIAL |
| 7-8 | A detailed and organised plan, prioritised with reasons, including the bench test, where every row lets you decide something. | EDITORIAL |

### Tips

1. Every row should let you decide something. If it does not, take it off the plan.
2. Spend the tokens on the bench test. It is the only thing that tells you what is physically possible.
3. Reading about a sensor is not the same as watching one fail on your desk.
4. Write down what you gave up, and what could go wrong because you skipped it.

## 3. Aiii — Take apart four products, without opening them

*Criterion A, Inquiring and analysing*

| Field | Value | |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 3 classes | FROM PAGE |
| Worked | On your own | FROM PAGE |
| Hand in | 4 Black Box Cards and a summary | FROM PAGE |
| Also submit | Bring the cards to class for the summary discussion. | EDITORIAL |
| Deadline wording | End of class 3 | EDITORIAL |

### Do

Required on the submission:

- What it is, who it is for, what it costs, and how it gets its power.
- What it must be sensing, and your evidence.
- What it must be deciding. Look for behaviour a direct wire from input to output could not explain.
- What it does back, and which output does it.
- Your block diagram: what goes in, what happens in the middle, what comes out, with real catalog parts named.
- Power: mains, batteries or rechargeable, and what that choice forced the designer to do.
- Controls and lights: every button and every indicator, and what a user must already know to use it.
- One sentence on what you would copy from this product, and what you would leave behind.

Class shape, for the strip under the masthead:

1. One card together, then your first one alone
2. Two more cards
3. The badly designed one, then your summary

### Hand in

- 4 Black Box Cards and a summary
- Submit to Photos of your four cards and a PDF of the summary to **ManageBac**
- Also submit: Bring the cards to class for the summary discussion.

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Analyses a range of existing products that inspire a solution to the problem in detail.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | An incomplete analysis, of products unlikely to guide your work. | EDITORIAL |
| 3-4 | Basic analysis of four products, though not all of them will help guide your work. | EDITORIAL |
| 5-6 | Partly detailed analysis of four products, most of which will help guide your work. | EDITORIAL |
| 7-8 | Detailed analysis of four products that all guide your work, with a summary that turns them into design implications. | EDITORIAL |

### Tips

1. You may share one product with a classmate, no more.
2. Do not open anything. Work out what is inside from how it behaves.
3. Name real catalog parts in your block diagrams, not just "a sensor".
4. The summary is where the marks are. Four cards and no summary stays at 5-6.

*Criterion A, Inquiring and analysing*

| Field | Value | |

| Worked | On your own | FROM PAGE |
| Hand in | Design Brief, 1 page | FROM PAGE |
| Also submit |  | TO WRITE |

### Do

Required on the submission:

- A summary of your research, not a repeat of it.
- Who the client is and what they need, carried over from Ai.
- What the device must do. This is the row that becomes your specification in Bi.
- The constraints you already know about: power, size, cost, where it lives.

### Hand in

- Design Brief, 1 page
- Submit to A PDF of your brief to **ManageBac**
- Also submit: *TO WRITE*

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Develops a detailed design brief, which summarizes the analysis of relevant research.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | An unfinished brief that does not reference your research. | EDITORIAL |
| 3-4 | A basic brief that references your research. | EDITORIAL |
| 5-6 | A brief that includes a summary of your research. | EDITORIAL |
| 7-8 | A detailed brief that summarises your research and states clearly what the device must do. | EDITORIAL |

### Tips

1. Referencing your research is a requirement, not an option.
2. "What the device must do" is the row that carries into Bi. Spend your time there.
3. Write about hardware here, not about people. You already did the people in Ai.
4. One page. If it runs longer, you are repeating your research instead of summarising it.

## 5. Bi — Write your specification

*Criterion B, Developing ideas*

| Field | Value | |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 1 class | FROM PAGE |
| Worked | On your own | FROM PAGE |
| Hand in | Design Specification, 1 page | FROM PAGE |
| Also submit |  | TO WRITE |
| Deadline wording | End of class 1 | EDITORIAL |

### Do

Required on the submission:

- One row per rule, each written so it can be tested.
- Numbers wherever a number is possible. Times, distances, durations, counts.
- The decision rule: what your device works out on its own, without being told.
- The standalone rule: working on your client's desk, on its own power, with nobody helping it.
- Where each rule came from in your research.

### Hand in

- Design Specification, 1 page
- Submit to A PDF of your specification to **ManageBac**
- Also submit: *TO WRITE*

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Develops detailed design specifications, which explain the success criteria for the design of a solution based on the analysis of the research.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | An incomplete set of specifications. | EDITORIAL |
| 3-4 | Basic specifications, partly linked to research, some of which can be tested. | EDITORIAL |
| 5-6 | Specifications partly linked to relevant research, which can be tested. | EDITORIAL |
| 7-8 | Detailed specifications, linked to relevant research and all testable, including the decision rule and the standalone power rule. | EDITORIAL |

### Tips

1. Every rule must be testable. If you cannot describe the test, rewrite the rule.
2. Put numbers in. "Fast" is not a specification, "within 2 seconds" is.
3. Include the rule about the decision your device makes on its own.
4. Include the rule about running on its own power with nobody helping it.

## 6. Bii — Come up with a range of ideas

*Criterion B, Developing ideas*

| Field | Value | |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 3 classes | FROM PAGE |
| Worked | On your own | FROM PAGE |
| Hand in | 6 ideas + 2 breadboard tests | FROM PAGE |
| Also submit | A photo or short video of each breadboard test, with a note on what it proved. | EDITORIAL |
| Deadline wording | End of class 3 | EDITORIAL |

### Do

Required on the submission:

- Block diagram. What goes in, what happens in the middle, what comes out, with real catalog parts named.
- Sketch of the object. Roughly to scale, in the place it will live, next to a hand or a desk for size.
- Three frame storyboard. What the user does, what the device does back, and what state it is left in.
- Your notes, explaining how this idea helps your client and pointing at your Bi rules by number, plus one risk.

Class shape, for the strip under the masthead:

1. Draw cards, make ideas fast
2. Develop and annotate your best six
3. Breadboard tests and the swap

### Hand in

- 6 ideas + 2 breadboard tests
- Submit to Photos of all six idea sheets to **ManageBac**
- Also submit: A photo or short video of each breadboard test, with a note on what it proved.

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Develops a range of feasible design ideas, using an appropriate medium(s) and detailed annotation, which can be correctly interpreted by others.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Fewer than six complete ideas, with messy drawings and few notes. | EDITORIAL |
| 3-4 | Six ideas with drawings and some notes. | EDITORIAL |
| 5-6 | Six ideas with clean block diagrams, sketches and notes. | EDITORIAL |
| 7-8 | Six well developed ideas with clean diagrams, storyboards and dense notes, plus two breadboard tests that prove the risky parts work. | EDITORIAL |

### Tips

1. Write far more notes than you think you need. You are explaining the idea to someone who is not you.
2. Use a ruler and drawing tools. Neat drawings are easier to annotate.
3. Build only the risky part on the breadboard. Twenty minutes is enough to kill a bad idea.
4. Six genuinely different ideas, not one idea in six colours.

1. Block diagram, circuit diagram, wiring list
2. State diagram and code plan
3. Case, parts list, requirements, test points

### Hand in

- Production Pack, 6 to 9 pages
- Submit to A single PDF of the whole pack to **ManageBac**
- Also submit: The pack must be signed off by Mr. K before build class 1.

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Develops accurate and detailed planning drawings/diagrams and outlines requirements for the creation of the chosen solution.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|

| 5-6 | Partly accurate and somewhat detailed drawings, with requirements outlined. | EDITORIAL |

- Also submit: *TO WRITE*

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Constructs a detailed and logical plan, which describes the efficient use of time and resources, sufficient for peers to be able to follow to create the solution.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|

## 11. Ciii — Follow your plan and finish it

*Criterion C, Creating the solution*

| Field | Value | |
|---|---|---|
| Time | End of the build classes | FROM PAGE |
| Worked | On your own | FROM PAGE |
| Hand in | The finished device | FROM PAGE |
| Also submit | The device itself, demonstrated in class and working on its own power. | EDITORIAL |
| Deadline wording | Last build class | EDITORIAL |

### Do

Required on the submission:

- It does what your specification says, including the decision it makes on its own.
- It runs on its own power, with no laptop connected, for as long as your specification claims.
- It is in a case, or deliberately open and neat, with every board fixed down and nothing loose.
- Every button and every light is labelled, in words your client can read.
- There is a way to turn it on and off.
- The wiring is tidy enough to repair, and connectors unplug instead of being cut.
- A one page user sheet: what it does, how to start it, what each light or sound means, and what to do when it stops.

### Hand in

- The finished device
- Submit to Photos of the finished device and a PDF of your user sheet to **ManageBac**
- Also submit: The device itself, demonstrated in class and working on its own power.

### Graded

MYP descriptor, quoted verbatim on the sheet:

> Follows the plan to create the solution, which functions as intended and is presented appropriately.

| Band | What it looks like | |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | A device that does not follow the plan and does not work. | EDITORIAL |
| 3-4 | A device that follows the plan loosely and works with several problems. | EDITORIAL |
| 5-6 | A device that follows the plan and works with few problems. | EDITORIAL |
| 7-8 | A device that follows the plan, does what your specification says including its own decision, runs on its own power, and is finished well enough to hand to your client. | EDITORIAL |

### Tips

1. Test it on its own power, with no laptop attached, before you call it finished.
2. Label every button and every light in words your client can read.
3. Connectors should unplug, not be cut. You will need to repair it.
4. Write the one page user sheet as if you will not be there to explain anything.

## 14. Dii — Test it and judge the results

*Criterion D, Evaluating*

| Field | Value | |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 2 classes | FROM PAGE |
| Worked | On your own | FROM PAGE |
| Hand in | Every rule, judged one by one | FROM PAGE |
| Also submit | Your completed test sheets, photographed or scanned. | EDITORIAL |
| Deadline wording | End of class 2 | EDITORIAL |

### Do

Required on the submission:
