# DP Design Technology Remix

**Status:** first draft for discussion, 9 October 2026. Nothing here changes the existing topic pages.

This document proposes a reorganised way through the DP Design Technology syllabus (first assessment 2027). It regroups the 24 sub-topics into 12 units so that each idea is taught once, in the place where it makes most sense to a student, while every understanding in the guide is still covered. It also sets out where the recommended teaching hours go under the new grouping, and gives each unit a set of value measures so that students and teachers can see where exam marks have actually been concentrated.

The existing pages (`a1.1-ergonomics.html` and the rest) stay as they are, apart from ordinary revisions. The remix is meant to run alongside them. A teacher could use it as a teaching order, as a revision route, or as a full replacement for the textbook sequence.

Sources, in order of authority:

1. `officialdocs/design-technology-guide-en.pdf`: understandings, "students must be able to" statements, recommended hours, linking questions and IA criteria.
2. `officialdocs/Exemplar Exams.pdf`: the HL and SL specimen papers 1 and 2. Every item was mapped to a guide understanding for the value measures in section 5. The mapping is in Appendix B.
3. The 24 topic pages in `curriculum/dp/`, read in full (course notes only, not quizzes or Paper 2 practice).
4. `samplepapers/paper1.md` and `samplepapers/paper2.md`, for how hours and specimen coverage have been used to weight papers so far.

---

## 1. Summary

- About three quarters of the guide's understandings (124 of 161) share their content with at least one understanding in another sub-topic. At SL the figure is 66 of 79. This is mostly the guide's own structure: the A strand introduces an idea, the B strand applies it, and the C strand puts it in context, but each strand restates the idea in full.
- The pages inherit that repetition and add some of their own. Research methods are taught on three pages. SLA, FDM and SLS are described on three pages. The triple bottom line is explained in five places across three pages. Where two pages teach the same thing, they often frame it differently (section 3.1).
- The proposed remix has **12 units**: 7 that SL students take (with HL extensions attached to three of them) and 5 that are HL only. The SL/HL boundary is kept intact, so an SL student can skip every HL block without missing anything they are examined on.
- Teaching once instead of twice or three times should release roughly **12 to 18 hours at SL and 30 to 40 hours at HL** from the 90 and 180 recommended content hours. These are planning estimates. Section 6 suggests where the time goes, mostly into the IA and practical work.
- Exam marks in the specimen papers do not follow the hour allocations. The 2.2 hours that cover senses and usability objectives (A1.1.7, B1.1.4) attracted 10 specimen items across the four papers. The 16 hours of electronics attracted 3 marks. The 42 hours of modelling and prototyping attracted about 19 % of SL marks, which is consistent with most of that time being practical. Section 5 turns this into a set of measures per unit.

## 2. How the remix was built

Each of the 161 understandings was assigned to exactly one remix unit (Appendix A). The rules were:

- An understanding moves to wherever its idea is best taught once. For example, B2.1.7 (product analysis as a tool) moves into the product analysis unit with C3.1, because they are the same activity.
- SL content is never placed inside an HL unit. Where an SL understanding and an HL understanding overlap, the SL one becomes the main teaching point and the HL one becomes an extension that builds on it. A3.1.2 (frame, shell and solid structures, SL) is taught in the materials unit, so A3.2.2 (the same classification, HL) needs no new teaching.
- The guide's highest command term wins. When A3.3.9 asks students to *identify* levers and B3.3.6 asks them to *calculate* load and effort, the merged lesson teaches to *calculate*. Merging never reduces what a student must be able to do.
- Hours follow the understandings. A unit inherits each sub-topic's recommended hours in proportion to the number of understandings it takes from that sub-topic. This is crude, since understandings differ in size, but it is transparent and it adds up exactly to 90 (SL) and 180 (HL).

### 3.2 Why this matters to students

The repetition does two kinds of damage. The first is time: a concept taught three times costs three lessons. The second is trust. When a student meets the same idea on a second page, written differently and sometimes with different facts, the reasonable conclusion is that the two are different ideas, or that one of them is wrong. The two five-stage models, the three lists of five qualities, and the two efficiency formulas are the clearest cases. Students are not told why the material is spread around, because there is no reason a student would accept: the split follows the guide's A/B/C architecture rather than anything about how the ideas work.

### 3.3 Uneven care

Reading all 24 pages in one sitting makes the differences in depth and polish visible in a way that ongoing revision does not. These are the ones that matter most for a remix, because a remix has to pick one version of each idea. The full list is in Appendix C.

- Statement wording had drifted from the guide on 20 pages. Comparing every understanding and "students must" line with the guide found 77 that differed in wording, plus 19 that differed only in spelling or quotation marks. Most changes were small (a dropped clause, "SF" written out, "VR/AR" abbreviated), but some removed content, and on A3.4 eight of fourteen statements (3.4.7 to 3.4.14) had been rewritten to describe different content. All statements that dropped or changed content were restored to the guide wording on 9 October, keeping British spelling (`statement-audit.md`).
- Restoring the statements shows where the notes have not kept up. A3.4 never compares open- and closed-loop systems, does not cover signal conditioning against program control, and teaches op-amp gain formulas instead of op-amps in IoT appliances. A4.1.10 names sealants (oils, wax, silicone) that the finishing table does not cover. These, and a handful of smaller cases, are listed under "Notes to update" in `statement-audit.md`.
- B2.2 is thin for its weight. It carries 30 of the 90 SL hours, a third of the SL course, yet its notes (about 2 900 words) are shorter than those for A2.2 (about 3 600), the 10-hour topic it largely repeats. Most of B2.2's hours are meant to be practical, so a short page is defensible, but the page does not say so and does not structure the practical time.

### 3.4 Hours and exam weight do not line up

The guide's hours tell us how long the IB thinks a topic takes to teach. They do not tell us how many marks it carries. The specimen papers, small sample though they are, show large differences.

## 5. Value measures

### 5.1 What each measure means

I have used four measures and a combined priority tier. Each one answers a different question, and none of them should be read alone.

| Measure | Question it answers | How it is calculated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Specimen marks** | How much of a real paper did this unit carry? | Every item in the four specimen papers was mapped to one guide understanding (Appendix B). Marks are summed per unit. SL papers total 80 marks; HL papers total 120. |
| **Exam yield** | Does this unit earn more or fewer marks than its teaching time would suggest? | Share of specimen marks divided by share of guide hours. 1.0 means marks and hours are in proportion. Above 1 is a good return on study time. |
| **Connections** | How likely is this unit to turn up as the second or third angle in a multi-topic case study? | The number of the guide's linking questions, in other units, that point at this unit's sub-topics. Paper 1 case studies span at least three sub-topics each, so a well-connected unit gets pulled into other units' questions. |
| **Inclusion rating** | How likely is the unit to appear in a given exam session? | A 1 to 5 judgement from the three measures above plus the paper structure. 5 means it appeared in every specimen paper at that level or is built into the paper's structure. 3 means it appeared, but in small or single items. |

## 6. Sequence and where the released time goes

### 6.1 A suggested order

The order follows the IA, because the IA is the one piece of work every student does in full and its criteria follow the design process.

| When | Units | Why here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Year 1, first term | R2, R1, R3 | Users and bodies first. This gives students what they need for IA criterion A (persona, storyboard) and the language for analysing products. |
| Year 1, second term | R4, R6, R10 | Product analysis, problem statements and specifications (IA criteria A and B), with materials and sustainability as the main lenses for analysing a product. |
| Year 1, third term | HL: R7, R8. SL: studio and IA time | Structures and mechanisms at HL, while students are starting to model their own ideas. SL students have no new content units here, which frees this term for the IA. |
| Year 2, first term | HL: R9, R11, R12; HL extensions of R3, R4, R10. SL: IA and case study practice | Electronics and making, then the HL extensions, which all build on Year 1 units. |
| Year 2, second term | Review against the value measures | Revision weighted by tier. |
| Throughout | R5 studio thread | Weekly studio time, timed so that drawing, CAD and prototyping skills arrive just before IA criteria C, D and E need them. |

### 6.2 Where the released time could go

- The IA should get most of it. The guide allows 50 hours for the design project. Released content time can sit beside it as supervised studio time, especially for the persona, storyboard and product analysis work in criterion A, which carries 9 of the 33 IA marks, more than any other criterion.
- Every Paper 1 and Paper 2 question sits on a case study. A weekly case study, using the subject bank in `samplepapers/paper1.md` §4.6, would practise exactly the skill the papers test: applying several units to one product.
- Physical testing for R6 and R7, mechanism builds for R8 and circuit builds for R9 turn calculation into something students can see and handle.
- Some of the time should simply go back to reviewing the units in the Core tier.

## 7. My thoughts

### 7.1 This is worth doing

The repetition is real, it is large, and it costs students time and clarity. The guide's A/B/C structure makes sense to the people who wrote it, as a map of theory, practice and context. It does not make sense to a 16-year-old who has just been taught the same research methods for the third time. A remix that teaches each idea once, in a place where its purpose is obvious, is a better course. The SL/HL split survives intact, which was the main structural risk.

### 7.2 Build it without duplicating the pages

The biggest practical risk is maintenance. If the remix is built as twelve new pages that copy content out of the existing twenty-four, every future correction has to be made twice, and the two versions will drift apart the way the duplicated content inside the current pages already has.

I would build each remix unit as a hub page that pulls its sections from the existing pages instead of copying them. The pages already give every understanding an anchor (`#obj-3.2.1` and so on), so a remix page could fetch the source page and insert the chosen `obj-section` blocks at runtime with `fetch` and `DOMParser`. That fits the no-bundler architecture, needs no build step, and means a fix on `a3.2-structural-systems.html` appears in R7 automatically. The remix page itself would only hold what is new: the guiding question, the bridging text that explains why these ideas sit together, the consolidated comparison tables (research methods, the three lists of five qualities, the two five-stage models), and the value measures.

Where the existing pages hold two versions of the same idea, the hub page chooses one. That choice is where the remix does its real work, and it is a good prompt to fix the weaker version on the original page at the same time.

### 7.3 Things to decide before building

- Students need to recognise the guide codes, because the guide, the textbook and other schools' materials use them. Every remix unit should list the guide codes it covers, and every embedded section should keep its original code visible.
- The remix teaches to the highest command term when two understandings merge. That should be stated on each unit page, so a student reading A3.3.9 ("identify") inside R8 knows the bar is B3.3.6 ("calculate").
- The teacher-first presentation decks are built per topic page. A remix deck could be assembled from the same `data-ps` hints in the embedded sections, but this needs checking against how presentation mode collects its slides.
- For a pilot, I would start with R2. It is SL, it has the worst triplication (three pages, three method lists, three persona treatments), it feeds IA criterion A directly, and it is small enough to build and time in a few lessons. R7 and R8 would be a good HL pilot for a different reason: two pages each and a clean A-then-B overlap.

### 7.4 What the remix cannot fix

The remix cannot tell us how the IB will actually weight things in May 2027. The value measures rest on one specimen per level, and the first live papers may look quite different. It also cannot remove the guide's internal duplications for teachers who use the textbook, which follows the guide's order. A teacher using both will need the mapping in Appendix A, which is why it is there.
