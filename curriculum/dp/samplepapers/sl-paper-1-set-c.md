**2.** Osprey manufactures backpacks for hiking. Its Anti-Gravity suspension, introduced in 2014, runs a single piece of tensioned mesh continuously from the lumbar pad around to the hip belt, holding the body of the pack away from the wearer's back.

A loaded pack can weigh 15 kg or more. The hip belt is shaped to transfer most of that weight onto the pelvis rather than leaving it on the shoulders.

Osprey's All Mighty Guarantee undertakes to repair any Osprey pack of any age free of charge, whatever caused the damage.

## C. SL scope verification

Excluded because it is **HL only**:

- **A3.4 and B3.4 Electronic systems.** The Echo is a microphone array, a processor and a network connection, and the Joy-Con stick is a potentiometer. Neither is asked about as an electronic system. Q6 is written on usability, capability and designer responsibility; Q7c asks about the wear of a contact surface as a material property, not about the circuit that reads it. This is the sharpest scope risk in the paper.
- **A3.2, A3.3 and B3.2, B3.3.** No structural or mechanical systems item. The Osprey suspension is a tensioned structure and is deliberately asked about on ergonomics and material selection rather than on forces.
- **A4.1, B4.1 and C4.1 Production.** Moulding and tooling are named as facts in Q10g and Q10h, but no item asks the candidate to classify a manufacturing technique, compare a production system, or apply a design for manufacture strategy. Q10h is written on the advantage of prototyping before tooling, which is A2.2.
- **C1.3 Beyond usability.** The Labubu blind-box case is a direct invitation to the four-pleasure framework, and Q9 is deliberately written on C1.1 and C3.1 instead. Collectables and designed scarcity are the single easiest way to leak HL content into an SL paper.
- **C3.2 Life-cycle analysis.** Q10i is answered on Datschefski's principles, which is C2.1, not on life-cycle stages.
