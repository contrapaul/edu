## C. SL scope verification

Excluded because it is **HL only**:

- **A3.3 and B3.3 Mechanical systems.** The Leatherman is a nest of pivoting tools and the Owala lid is a sprung latch, and neither is asked about as a mechanism. Q3 is written on material properties and circular economy, and Q5b on the ergonomics of one-handed operation rather than on the linkage that achieves it. This is the sharpest scope risk in the paper.
- **A3.2 and B3.2 Structural systems.** A bicycle frame is the standard structural case and Q6 deliberately asks nothing about forces, triangulation or stiffness as a structural property. Q6a is written on material selection factors, which is B3.1.
- **A3.4 and B3.4 Electronic systems.** Arduino is a microcontroller board and the Steam Deck is a computer. No part asks about inputs, processing, outputs or circuits. Q4 is written entirely on usability and research, and Q7 on prototyping, circular economy and responsibility.
- **A4.1, B4.1 and C4.1 Production.** Vulcanizing, moulding and frame building are named as facts, but no part asks the candidate to classify a manufacturing technique, compare a production system or apply a design for manufacture strategy. Q6c asks for a sustainability benefit of shared manufacture, which is C2.1, not B4.1.
- **C1.3 Beyond usability.** The Owala lid and the Leatherman are both products bought partly for how they feel to own, and the four-pleasure framework is the obvious tool. Q3 and Q5 are written on C2.2, A3.1 and B1.1 instead.
- **C3.2 Life-cycle analysis.** Q7d compares two products over many years and is answered on repair, parts supply and product analysis, not on life-cycle stages.
