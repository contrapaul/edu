## C. Calculation items

Three items require a calculator: **8b** (belt drive output speed), **9b** (safety factor), **10b** (electrical power).

This is a deliberate departure from the specimen, and it is worth being explicit about why. **The HL specimen Paper 1 contains no calculation at all**, yet its cover sheet states that a calculator is required, and B3.2, B3.3 and B3.4 each set out calculation explicitly as something students must be able to do: safety factors and Young's modulus, gear and belt ratios and mechanical advantage, and V = IR and P = VI.

Two readings are possible. Either the specimen simply did not sample that content, or calculation is reserved for Paper 2. The first is more likely, since a calculator would not be required for a paper containing no arithmetic. Three items out of forty hedges the uncertainty: enough that students meet the format, few enough that the paper is not miscalibrated if the real Paper 1 turns out to be calculation-free.

Each of the three is answerable in well under a minute and has distractors built from the specific errors students make:

| Item | Working | Distractor logic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8b | 2400 × (6 ÷ 30) = 480 rev/min | A: divides by the pulley diameter. C: assumes no change. D: multiplies instead of divides. |
| 9b | SF = 2400 ÷ 800 = 3 | A: inverts the ratio. B: guesses the conventional "1.5". D: subtracts. |
| 10b | P = VI = 3.0 × 0.020 = 60 mW | B: uses one cell, missing that series cells add. A and D: decimal slips. |

## F. Duplication check against the other Set A papers

That is the specimen's own practice: the HL and SL specimen Paper 1s share their opening five items outright, and the HL and SL Paper 2s share five of seven case studies.
