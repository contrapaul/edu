**2.** USB (Universal Serial Bus) is a connector and communication standard first published in 1996 by a group of manufacturers who agreed to replace the many incompatible ports then in use on computers.

The original USB-A connector could be inserted only one way round, and its correct orientation was difficult to see. The USB-C connector, published in 2014, is symmetrical, so it can be inserted either way up. **4.** The Sony TPS-L2, sold from 1979 as the Walkman, was the first widely available personal cassette player. It measured approximately 88 mm × 134 mm × 29 mm, ran on two AA batteries and was carried on a belt clip or in a coat pocket.

It had no recording function and no speaker. Sony's own engineers had argued that a cassette player without a recording function would not sell. It was fitted with two headphone sockets so that two people could listen at once.

More than 200 million cassette Walkmans were sold before the format was displaced.

## C. SL scope verification

Excluded because it is **HL only**:

- **C1.3 Beyond usability.** The four-pleasure framework and the ACT model do not appear. Both are used in the HL specimen Paper 2 (Q3d and Q6d) and both are HL only. This is the easiest scope error to make when writing a Paper 2 about user experience, because those two frameworks are the obvious tools to reach for.
- **A3.2, A3.3, A3.4 and B3.2, B3.3, B3.4.** No structural, mechanical or electronic systems item. Q6 is about a keyboard containing springs and a printed circuit board, and deliberately asks nothing about the spring, the circuit or the switch mechanism. Its questions are drawings, material selection, physiology and circular economy.
- **A4.1 and B4.1.** No manufacturing technique classification and no production systems item. Q6 mentions moulding as a fact about the case study but does not ask candidates to classify or compare processes.
- **C3.2 Life-cycle analysis.** Q6d is answered on C2.2 circular economy strategies, not on LCA stages.
- **C4.1 Design for manufacture strategies.** Q6d is phrased on "circular economy approach", not on design for disassembly as a DFM strategy, even though the exploded drawing invites it.

One note on terminology: the HL Paper 1 specimen uses *affordance*, *constraints*, *mapping* and *low memory burden* as product-user interface terms. None of these appears in the 2027 guide. They are not used here.

## E. Assessment objective split

By command term, following the guide's own AO groupings:

| | Items | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AO1 + AO2 | 1a, 1b, 2a, 2b, 2c, 2d, 3a, 3b, 4a, 4b, 5a, 5b, 6a, 6b, 6c, 7a, 7b, 7c | 30 (60%) |
| AO3 | 1c, 3c, 4c, 5c, 6d, 7d | 20 (40%) |

**This is 60/40, not the 50/50 the guide states, and that is deliberate: the SL specimen distributes exactly the same way.** Run the same mapping over the specimen and it also gives 30 marks of AO1/AO2 and 20 of AO3, because a [1][2][3] case is 3/3, a [1][1][2][2] case is 6/0, and a [2][2][2][4] case is 6/4.

The gap is a classification question rather than a structural one. The IB evidently counts some [2] items toward AO3, which is reasonable for items that require inference from supplied data rather than retrieval. Reclassifying the three clearest of those here (2c inclusive design reasoning, 5b matching changes to capabilities, 7b interpreting survey data) moves 6 marks and gives 24/26, close to 50/50.

The practical instruction: **match the specimen's tariff structure and the AO weighting looks after itself.** Do not distort the tariff ladder trying to hit 50/50 on a command-term count.

## G. Building the HL version

Per [paper2.md §3.1](../paper2.md), an HL Set A would reuse five of these case studies unchanged with different question parts, swap two for HL-content ones, and add the 30-mark Design Thinking Challenge.

- **Keep and rewrite the parts for:** Q1 student project, Q2 USB, Q5 WeChat, Q6 mechanical keyboards, Q7 World of Warcraft.
- **Swap out** Q3 Instagram and Q4 Sony Walkman, which carry no HL content.
- **Swap in** two cases that do. Portable power banks would serve A3.4 and B3.4 electronic systems; a load-bearing physical product would serve A3.2 and B3.2 structural systems. The mechanical keyboard case can also be pushed to HL by asking about the switch as a mechanical system (A3.3) and the PCB matrix as an electronic system (A3.4).
- **Add** an HL Design Thinking Challenge on a separate humanitarian or civic scenario, built to the twelve-step spine in [paper2.md §6.2](../paper2.md). None of the subjects in this pool suits it; the challenge needs a resource-constrained community problem with personas, a constraints table and a quantified materials list.
