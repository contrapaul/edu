**7.** In 2012 a teenager with cerebral palsy wrote to Nike asking for a shoe he could put on without help. Nike's response became the FlyEase range.

## C. SL scope verification

Excluded because it is **HL only**:

- **A3.2, A3.3, A3.4 and B3.2, B3.3, B3.4.** The Super Cub is a motor vehicle and the Aeron has a tilt mechanism, and neither is asked about as a structural, mechanical or electronic system. Q10 deliberately asks about the step-through frame as an ergonomic feature and the leg shield as a material, not about the engine or the centrifugal clutch. The clutch is mentioned once as a fact about how the machine is ridden, which is a user-need statement, and no item depends on it.
- **A4.1 and B4.1.** Die-cast zinc, moulding and pressing are named as facts about how components are made, but no item asks the candidate to classify or compare a manufacturing technique or a production system. Q9 is about material cost and assembly cost, which is B3.1 material selection, not A4.1.
- **C1.3 Beyond usability.** The four-pleasure framework and the ACT model do not appear. The KitchenAid colour range and the Aeron are both natural invitations to reach for them, and both items are deliberately written on C1.1 obsolescence and A1.1 ergonomics instead. This is the easiest scope error to make in a paper about desirable objects.
- **C3.2 Life-cycle analysis.** Q10h is answered on the triple bottom line, which is C2.1, not on life-cycle stages.
- **C4.1 Design for manufacture strategies.** Q9c is phrased on material selection and assembly cost, not on design for assembly, even though the moulded-on clothing invites it.

In a multiple-choice paper the AO3 items are those requiring the candidate to rank competing options, judge a trade-off, or infer from the case rather than retrieve a term.
