Daily Plans

Forty classes each, for the Grade 9 tabletop game design unit and the Grade 10 electronics design unit.

One row per class. Each row states the goal for that class, summarises what runs in it, names the assessment task being worked on, and lists anything that has to exist before the class starts. Both units are written as sixteen assessed tasks against the sixteen MYP Design strands, so the assessment column names strands rather than criteria.

Length

40 classes per unit

Assessed tasks

16, one per strand

Deadlines

Every task is due the day after the class it finishes in

Build blocks

10 classes plus one rescue class

Client contact

G9 one session, G10 two sessions

Slack

3 classes held at the end of each unit

Both units are published as thirty six class sequences. These plans run to forty, and the four extra classes are placed where the units are most likely to slip: one reteach class before the production drawings in Grade 9, one rescue class at the end of each build block, and slack at the end for lost classes, a showcase and handover.

Deadlines. A task is due the day after the class it finishes in. That is why the assessment column reads "in class" for the classes where work is produced, and "due" only on the last class of that task. Nothing carries a deadline that lands on a class day.

Ongoing tasks. Cii, Ciii and Civ run through the whole build block in both units. They appear on every build row, because they are collected in class rather than written afterwards. The five minute log stop at the end of every build class is what makes that work.

The notes column carries what has to be ready before the class starts: materials, machines, bookings, and the tools and activities that class depends on. Anything marked planned does not exist yet and the class runs on paper until it does.

Teams of three or four build one original tabletop game for a client group inside the school, then test it with that group. Every student is assessed individually on a shared build, which is why component ownership is fixed in Biv and never moves afterwards.

Classes 1 to 7 · Criterion A

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
1 Teams formed, clients assigned, both non-negotiables understood.Unit shape explained as sixteen separate tasks with sixteen deadlines. Teams of three or four are formed, then each team is assigned a client group by the teacher, based on what the team says it wants to make. The two rules are taught: meaningful choices, and a session that fits inside a recess. The four tests of a meaningful choice are walked through against a game on the table, and the roll and move trap is named now rather than in Dii. None. Launch class. Game Mechanics Catalog introduced. Class game sets on the tables. Running portfolio started. Client teachers confirmed before this class.
2 Argue that the assigned client group needs a game.Need or want sort in pairs, then the weak brief autopsy so students see justification by its absence. The need statement is drafted in class: who the audience is in specifics, the situation now with at least one piece of gathered evidence, why a tabletop game beats a club, an app or nothing, and what happens if nothing is made. Ai Need Statement, one page, due the day after this class. Both activities are live. The word "I" is banned from the first draft. Evidence has to be gathered, not assumed, so the client teacher must be reachable this week.
3 A prioritised research plan built inside the twelve token budget.Spend the tokens as a practice run against the Grade 12 common room brief, then the question quality ladder. Students then build the real plan, one row per activity, eight to twelve rows, and write the two paragraphs underneath on why this order and what was given up. Aii Research Plan, prioritised, due the day after this class. Research budget planner is live and holds twelve tokens. The "what decision this unlocks" column is the graded one, so any row that unlocks nothing comes off the plan.
4 See a teardown modelled, then complete one alone.A full teardown is led on the board, thinking aloud and including the parts that are uncertain. Mechanic spotting speed run follows as a race to build shared vocabulary. Students then complete teardown one on a game played in class. Aiii in progress. Nothing handed in this class. Aiii Teardown Sheet is live. Game Mechanics Catalog required. Physical game copies for every table.
5 Two more teardowns, covering the required range.Teardown two uses a mechanic family the student has not used before. Teardown three is free choice and may be a video game if the mechanic transfers to a table. Team overlap is checked: no more than one of a student's three games may also be analysed by a teammate. Aiii in progress. Students without a physical copy work from the catalog, publisher rules and video. Overlap check happens at the start of class, not the end.
6 Turn three teardowns into numbered design implications.The comparison matrix is built across all three games against the same criteria, then the implications are written in the form "Because..., my game should...". These get quoted directly in Aiv and Bi, so the class ends with them sharpened rather than listed. Aiii, three teardowns plus the synthesis page, due the day after this class. This class decides the grade for the whole task. Three good teardowns with no synthesis caps at 5-6, so the matrix cannot be left as homework.
7 Pull the research into one brief with an evidence column.Brief or spec sort first, ten statements, which fixes the most common A to B confusion before it costs marks twice. Then the one page brief: audience, the need restated in a sentence, three to five findings, the design implications carried over, the constraints, and the design intent paragraph. Aiv Design Brief, one page, due the day after this class. The evidence column is required on every row. Optional back of the box version of the design intent for anyone who finishes early. Measurable criteria stay out of this page.

Classes 8 to 17 · Criterion B

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
8 Write a specification every line of which can be tested.Make it measurable runs first, starting with "the game is fun" so the hardest case is dealt with in public. Spec to test matching follows. Students then write eight to twelve lines across the four columns, including the how it will be tested column, with at least one line in each required category and one line each for the two non-negotiables. Bi Specification, eight to twelve testable lines, due the day after this class. Specification builder is planned, so this class runs on the four column table on paper. Required categories: audience fit, meaningful choice, playtime, rules clarity, components, balance, aesthetics, accessibility, cost.
9 Generate quantity before quality.Two family challenge as a warm up, three rounds of eight minutes. Then the real mechanic draw: two family cards from the catalog per student, three draws, three fast concepts, no annotation and no neatness. The first round is expected to be poor and students are told so in advance. Bii in progress. Mechanic family cards cut and shuffled before class. A3 paper. Meaningful choice checker is planned, so the four tests are run verbally.
10 Annotate six concepts to the standard another designer could build from.Annotation density check first, two sketches of the same idea, one bare and one annotated. Then the best six are developed on A3 with all five required elements: core loop diagram, component sketch roughly to scale, mechanics named from the catalog, the meaningful choice circled and labelled, a link to a Bi line by number, and one risk. Bii in progress. Annotation is the assessed part, not the drawing. Any concept where the meaningful choice cannot be pointed at is not finished.
11 Prove feasibility by playing it, then prove the drawings can be read.Two paper prototypes built from scrap card and played for five minutes each. Interpretation rehearsal runs first as an ungraded pass. Then the real swap: a classmate explains your concept back from the sheet alone while you stay silent, and you write down everything they got wrong. Bii, six to eight annotated concepts, two played prototypes and the interpretation list, due the day after this class. Scrap card, dice, counters and blank cards on every table. The list of misreadings is submitted, since it is the only direct evidence for "correctly interpreted by others".
12 Converge as a team, then score the finalists honestly.The rigged matrix activity first, where the highest scoring option is obviously wrong, which stops blind trust in a number. Each student pitches their strongest concept, the team converges on one direction, and each student builds a weighted decision matrix scoring three finalists against every Bi line, with the weights justified. Biii in progress. Students whose concept was not chosen still write the justification, and that is stated when the task is set so it does not read as a consolation prize.
13 Write a justification that argues against its own choice.Argue against something you like, as a low stakes rehearsal. Then the individual write up of one to two pages: why the chosen design wins with Bi lines cited by number, the case against it, what was carried over from the rejected concepts, and what is still unresolved. Biii, decision matrix and critical justification, due the day after this class. A justification with no acknowledged weakness caps at 5-6 however well written, so the case against gets checked before students leave.
14 Bring back scale, dimensioning and title blocks before the pack starts.Dimension a real card: take a card from a published game, measure it, draw it to scale with bleed and safe margin marked. Then manifest a published game, opening a real box and writing its complete component manifest. Students consistently underestimate how many distinct parts a small game has. None. Reteach class, no submission. Published game boxes, rulers, calipers, drawing paper. This is the extra class added to the published sequence, and skipping it moves the cost into Biv.
15 Component manifest and dimensioned board drawing.Every component listed with quantity, size in millimetres, material and named owner. Then the board drawn to scale, dimensioned, with a title block. Ownership is decided here and it does not move afterwards, because Biv and Ci must name the same person for the same parts. Biv in progress. Ownership list is copied straight into Ci. Every student must own components, and any student owning none is flagged in this class.
16 Card, piece and icon drawings at final size.Card template dimensioned with bleed, safe margin and type sizes. Token and piece drawings, orthographic, or a flat pattern where a part folds, with the model shown for anything 3D printed. Iconography sheet drawn at final size with the meaning of every icon written next to it. Biv in progress. 3D printers and the small laser etcher are reliable. The large laser cutter is not, so any part that depends on it needs a stated fallback on the requirements page.
17 Finish the pack and settle everything with a lead time.Rulebook map covering section structure and page order across the four pages, then the requirements page: materials, tools, quantities, cost and time per component, plus the fallback for anything routed through the large laser cutter. Print shop work is finalised in this class because it costs money and takes time. Biv Production Pack, four to eight pages, due the day after this class. Hard ordering deadline. Anything going to the print shop is finished by the end of this class or it does not happen. The rulebook map is structure, not prose.

Classes 18 to 30 · Criterion C

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
18 A build plan a stranger could follow.Follow a bad plan, then the critical path hunt across ten dependent tasks. Students write the team plan and their own personal task plan, then swap with another team for the peer follow test: the other team reads for five minutes and narrates what they would do in build class 1 and build class 4. Every gap is recorded, then the plan is revised. Ci, team plan and personal plan, both versions before and after, due the day after this class. Build planner is planned, so this runs on paper. Both versions are submitted, since the revision is the evidence. The personal plan must be usable by a teammate if the student is absent.
19 Teach the skills before they are assessed.Rotating skill clinics, fifteen minutes each: cutting and scoring, bleed setup, print shop file export, and a 3D print tolerance test. The good failure demonstration runs at the end, showing a first attempt beside a fixed second one. None. No submission. Four stations set up before class. Print shop orders placed today at the latest. Students choose which three Cii skills they will evidence, across at least two tracks.
20 Build class 1. Everyone starts the components they own.Spec check opens the class. Students begin the parts named against them in Biv. The making log and the change log are set up in this class, and the five minute log stop runs before packing up for the first time. Cii, Ciii and Civ all open. Change log entry written in class. Four routines start today and run every build class: spec check, catalog callout, log stop, swap and read. Change log with real timestamps is planned, so logs are dated by hand.
21 Build class 2. First skill at depth, documented as it happens.Production work continues. Dated work in progress photographs taken with the student's own hand or initials visible on the part. First failed attempt is recorded rather than hidden, because excellent skill needs something to be excellent against. Cii making log, Civ change log, both in class. Camera or phone access confirmed. A clean photograph alone shows a good outcome, not skill, so the first attempt is required evidence.
22 Build class 3. Board construction and bulk component production.Board mounting, cutting and finishing. Jig making for anyone producing twenty identical parts. Print shop and laser jobs are chased, and any fallback written in Biv is triggered now rather than later. Cii, Civ in class. Art and craft supplies, cutting mats, steel rules. Check the large laser cutter queue at the start of class.
23 Build class 4. Second skill at depth, and the rules draft begins.Second skill track opened, so the two track minimum is met with time to spare. The rulebook draft starts against the four page limit, with structure, iconography and a worked example carrying the load instead of prose. Cii, Civ in class. OnePageRules handed out as the compression model. Iconography from Biv applied so the rulebook and the components agree.
24 Build class 5. Balance the game with numbers.Playtest inside the team against the playtime line in Bi, timed. Balancing done by counting rather than by feeling. Any dominant strategy found is logged as a change with its trigger, the options considered and the reason for the one chosen. Cii, Civ in class. Stopwatches. The coin flip test and the dominant strategy check are run on the team's own game in this class.
25 Build class 6. Third skill at depth.The last of the three skills is worked at depth, with the same evidence pattern: work in progress, outcome, and the note on what went wrong and what the second attempt did better. Students with fewer than three skills evidenced are named now, while there is still time. Cii, Civ in class. Mid block audit of the making log against the three skills and two tracks requirement.
26 Build class 7. Internal rulebook cold read.Draft rulebooks swapped between teams. Each team reads the other's rules and starts to set up, while the authors watch in silence. Every question asked and every rule misread is recorded, then fixed. Teams that skip this find their gaps during the client session, when it counts against them. Ciii evidence, Civ in class. Rulebooks must be printed before this class. Runs one full week before the deadline so there is time to act on it.
27 Build class 8. Components finished and assembled.All owned components completed and brought together as one box. Teaching the game in under five minutes is rehearsed inside the team. Anything still outstanding is listed against a named owner and a class number. Cii, Ciii, Civ in class. Box or storage for each game. Component manifest from Biv used as the checklist.
28 Build class 9. The cold read test.A team who has never seen the game receives the box and the rulebook and nothing else. They set up and play with no help of any kind. The designers watch in silence and record every question asked and every rule misread. A designer who explains has destroyed the data. Ciii cold read record, Civ in class. Pairing of teams arranged beforehand. This is also the rehearsal for Di, so the recording sheets are kept.
29 Build class 10. Fix what the cold read found, then finish.Fixes made and logged. Presentation completed: box, insert, component storage. Making log, change log and the game itself brought to a finished state and handed in. Cii making log, Ciii the game with its cold read record, and Civ change log due the day after this class for anyone not using the rescue class. Expect fifteen to thirty change log rows across the block. An undated log written in one sitting cannot reach the top band.
30 Rescue class. Close the gaps, hard cut off.Buffer for late print shop returns, failed prints, and any student who does not yet have three skills at depth across two tracks. Portfolio checked against the Biv ownership list so every student can be assessed individually on the shared build. Cii, Ciii and Civ due the day after this class for everyone. No extensions past this point. Added class. Teams already finished use it for the Di reading, so it is not idle time for them.

Classes 31 to 40 · Criterion D, and close

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
31 Give every specification line a method that could actually test it.Method matching across six specifications, with the one that cannot be tested by questionnaire argued out loud. Students then work down their own Bi page one row at a time, choosing method, participants, data type and success threshold, and checking that both qualitative and quantitative data appear. Di in progress. Methods are drawn from the DP user centred research toolkit, which sets up the DP course for anyone continuing. Playtime needs a stopwatch, not an opinion.
32 Build the instruments, not a list of intentions.Write five bad questions and fix them, then build an observation sheet against five minutes of play footage. Students then produce the real instruments: the questionnaire with its questions in order, the observation sheet with what is tallied, the interview script with follow ups, and the user trial protocol saying what is timed and by whom. Di Test Plan with the instruments attached, due the day after this class. Printing time needed at the end of class, since the instruments have to exist on paper for the client session. Naming a method is not designing one.
33 The client session. Collect real data from the real audience.Each team takes its game to its own client group. Every student runs their own instrument and collects their own data. Nobody helps the players understand the rules, because that is the whole test. Before leaving, each student writes three predicted unintended effects, to be checked against in Div. Dii data collection. No submission from this class. Room and client teacher booked well in advance. Stopwatches, printed instruments, clipboards. Students leave with sheets filled in, not with an impression.
34 Judge every specification line against the evidence.Met, partly met or not met, practised on shared data first, since students over claim "met" by a wide margin. Four reasons to be cautious follows. Then the point by point table, one row per Bi line with none skipped, and the closing section on the limits of the evidence. Dii, point by point evaluation, due the day after this class. The limits section is where "critically" lives. A student who admits three testers is thin shows more judgement than one who claims certainty, and that is rewarded plainly.
35 Improvements that trace back to evidence.Trace the improvement activity first, where students mark which of their own suggestions have no evidence behind them. Then five to eight ranked improvements, each naming the specification it addresses, the Dii finding that prompted it, why it would work, and its cost and feasibility. Diii, ranked improvements, due the day after this class. One improvement must address something that passed. An improvement with no evidence behind it is a preference and comes off the list.
36 Separate impact from satisfaction, then draft the report.Satisfaction or impact sort, ten statements, then the unintended effects hunt checked against the predictions written before the client session. Drafting begins across the six required sections, with the Ai need statement quoted directly rather than paraphrased. Div in progress. "They enjoyed it" is a Dii finding. Impact asks what the product did to the people it was made for and to anyone near them.
37 Finish the impact report and say it out loud.Pitch rehearsal in pairs, three minutes each, before anything is finalised. Then the report or poster is completed: who was affected, intended against actual, evidence from the session, unintended effects, wider impact across at least two of school, families, materials, cost and linguistic fit, and a plain answer on whether the need was met. Div, impact report as a poster or two pages, due the day after this class. Poster stock or two page layout decided in advance. Quoting the Ai need statement closes a loop that has been open for thirty six classes.
38 Audit the portfolio against all sixteen submissions.Each student checks the running portfolio against the sixteen strands, identifies missing or incomplete pieces, and completes anything that can still be completed. Component ownership is confirmed one last time so individual marks can be defended. Final portfolio due the day after this class. Bring the Biv ownership list, the making log and the change log to this class. No new task is set.
39 Showcase the games beyond the assigned client.Games are set up and played by other classes. Teams teach their game in under five minutes, which is the same skill rehearsed in build class 8. Feedback collected here is interesting but not assessed, and students are told that plainly. None. Not assessed. Space and timetable arranged. Optional if classes have been lost, since classes 39 and 40 are the slack.
40 Slack, reflection and handover.Held for lost classes anywhere in the unit. If not needed, it runs as reflection on the sixteen task structure, then games are returned to teams or archived for next year's Aiii teardowns. None. Keep two or three finished games for the class set. They are the best teardown material the next cohort will have.

Each student designs and builds one working electronic device for a real group in the school, outside their own grade, then tests it with them twice. Every task goes to ManageBac on completion. AI may be used for the code, and the marks sit in the four places it cannot reach: the state diagram, the code conversations, the bug diary, and the hardware.

Classes 1 to 3 · Bench block, not assessed

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
1 Client group chosen, board issued, first circuit working.Unit shape explained as sixteen tasks with sixteen deadlines. The two rules taught: it senses, decides and acts, and it works with nobody standing there. The AI position is stated in writing, including where the four sets of marks sit. Client groups are chosen from outside the student's own grade, with no more than two students per audience, decided inside this class. Bench drill 1 follows: an LED, a resistor and a breadboard, then the power measured with a multimeter. None. Launch class. One ESP32-S3 SuperMini per student, issued and recorded. Breadboards, jumper wires, resistors, LEDs, multimeters. Audience sign up sheet so the two per group cap is enforced in the room.
2 Sense, decide, act, felt in the hands.Bench drill 2. An input goes in, a light sensor or a button. An output comes out, an LED or a buzzer. In between the student sets a threshold, then changes it and watches the behaviour change. This is rule 1 of the unit, met physically before anyone has to write about it. None. Bench block is not marked. Light sensors, buttons, buzzers. Anyone who already knows this asks for the harder version rather than sitting through it.
3 Fault finding as a method, not as guessing.Bench drill 3. Four broken circuits and four broken pieces of code to diagnose. The five steps for finding a fault are taught and posted on the wall for the rest of the unit: what did I expect and what happened, is it power, wiring or code, what is the smallest test, change one thing and record it, then ask for help bringing all four answers. Bug diaries are opened. None. Bug diary starts here. Four pre broken stations prepared before class. Step 5 is also the rule for using AI, and that is said out loud in this class.

Classes 4 to 10 · Criterion A

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
4 Find a need that electronics is genuinely the right answer to.The sticky note test across ten real situations from around the school. Need, want or assumption sort. Then research with AI: what a group like this actually does all day, twenty problems worth solving, and an argument against the student's own favourite idea. Notes are taken throughout and kept. Ai research notes, handed in with the need statement. Laptops open in this class only. No component may be named in the first draft. If the need cannot be written without the word "sensor", it is a product advertisement.
5 Write the need statement by hand, from your own notes.Find the weak claims runs first, eight sentences with three unsupported claims to catch. Then laptops close and the need statement is written by hand in the room: the situation now with at least one piece of collected evidence, why this has to be electronic, and what happens if nobody makes anything. Ai Need Statement, handwritten in class, with the class 4 notes, due the day after this class. Added class, since Ai is written as two classes. Paper only. This is the fastest check on whether the research went into the student's head or only through their screen.
6 A research plan that spends twelve tokens on the right things.Question quality ladder, then what a bench test buys you, comparing two plans that both reach build class 4. Students then build the real plan in the planner, honouring both spending rules: at least four tokens on people, at least three on whether it will actually work. Aii Research Plan, one page, due the day after this class. Research budget planner is live and saves in the browser. Electronics Parts Catalog needed for part page rows. Bench test rows must name the part being tested.
7 Work out what is inside a product without opening it.One card done together on the board, out loud, including the parts that are uncertain, then checked against a repair video so students see what being wrong in public looks like. Name that part drill follows. Students then complete card 1 on something they can physically hold. Aiii in progress. Black Box Analyzer is live and draws the block diagram from the parts named. Electronics Parts Catalog required. A repair video queued up before class.
8 Cards 2 and 3, covering the required range.Card 2 is something made for the student's client group or usable by them. Card 3 has no screen, so it has to communicate with light, sound, movement or vibration. Guesses are named specifically enough to be wrong, then checked against a repair video or teardown page where one exists. Aiii in progress. Students who cannot get the real product work from the catalog, the product page, the manual and video. Being wrong and saying so scores better than staying vague.
9 The badly designed product, then the implications.Card 4 is chosen on purpose for being badly designed. Then the summary page compares all four against the same questions and ends in three to five numbered design implications in the form "Because..., my device should...", written carefully because they get quoted again in Aiv and Bi. Aiii, four Black Box Cards and the summary page, due the day after this class. Four good cards with no summary caps at 5-6. This class decides the grade for the whole task, so it cannot be rushed or left to homework.
10 Summarise the research into one brief with an evidence column.Brief or specification sort, ten statements, which fixes the most common A to B confusion. Then the one page brief: client, the need restated, three to five findings about people and about hardware, the design implications carried over, the what the device must do row, the limits, and the intent paragraph. Aiv Design Brief, one page, due the day after this class. The what the device must do row is the bridge to Bi, where it becomes numbers. Every row needs a source in the evidence column. Optional back of the box version for anyone finishing early.

Classes 11 to 19 · Criterion B

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
11 Turn the brief into rules with numbers in them.Make it measurable, then match the test to the rule, then find the untestable rule in someone else's specification. Students write eight to twelve rows with the rule, how they will know it passed, how it will be tested and where it came from, covering all nine required areas and including both mandatory rows for the two rules of the unit. Bi Design Specification, one page, due the day after this class. Specification builder is planned, so this runs on the four column table. Every named part must point at its catalog page. A specification missing either mandatory row comes back before it is marked.
12 Force variety before anything gets polished.Double draw sprint: an input card and an output card drawn, an idea sketched in eight minutes, three rounds. Coverage is checked at the end of class against the requirement of at least three different input rows and three different output rows across all ideas. Bii in progress. Input and output card decks prepared. This is what stops six versions of the first thing the student thought of.
13 Six ideas, annotated so a reader could wire them.Block diagram drill first, six devices described and diagrammed, each harder than the last. Then the best six go onto the A3 template with all four required parts in the same place every time: block diagram with real catalog parts and a middle box saying what gets decided, object sketch to scale in the place it will live, three frame storyboard, and the notes with a Bi reference by number plus one named risk. Bii in progress. A3 template printed. Writing "ESP32" in the middle box tells the reader nothing, so the decision has to be spelled out. This is the class where the notes get written.
14 Test the two riskiest ideas, then test whether the drawings can be read.Twenty minutes on each of the two riskiest parts on a breadboard: the sensor that might not detect what is needed, the screen that might be too slow, the servo that might be too weak. Then interpretation rehearsal, then the real swap, where a partner names the pin for every part and what the code checks first, using the block diagram alone and in silence. Bii, six labelled ideas, two breadboard tests and the interpretation list, due the day after this class. Breadboards and the sensor and output range available. A failed bench test is worth full marks, because it proves the idea now instead of in build class 4.
15 Kill at least one idea with a check rather than an opinion.Pin collision hunt and break the power budget run first, on someone else's design. Then all three checks are run on the student's three best ideas: pins assigned specifically using the catalog pin tables and the MacroPad Builder, worst case current added up against what the supply can give, and every part confirmed as present, in the catalog, or buyable with a price and a delivery time. Biii in progress. Electronics Parts Catalog pin tables, MacroPad Builder, multimeters. Expect at least one idea to die in this class, which is the point of checking before choosing.
16 Score the finalists, then argue against the winner.The rigged scoring table, then argue against something you like. Students score three ideas against every Bi row with weights they set and explain, then write one to two pages: why this one wins with rules cited by number, the argument against it, what was taken from the rejected ideas, and what is still unsolved. Anyone wanting a board other than the SuperMini argues for it here, using the three checks. Biii, the three checks, the scoring table and the reasons, due the day after this class. The weights get marked, not the arithmetic. A scoring table that only confirms what the power check already ruled out is doing no work, and saying so is the judgement this task wants.
17 The electrical half of the production pack.Circuit diagram both directions drill, building from a diagram and drawing from a built breadboard. Then the final block diagram, the circuit diagram with proper symbols and every value, and the wiring list naming board pin, part, signal type, wire colour and any resistor in the line. Soldering lesson 1 runs at the bench for six at a time. Biv in progress. Soldering lesson 1: iron care, tinning, heat and time, and what a good joint looks like next to a cold one and a bridge. Signed off on five joints on scrap board.
18 The behaviour half. Draw every mode before any code exists.Three state diagram examples shown first: a two mode device, one with a settings mode, and one that gives up and sleeps. State diagram drill on a microwave, traffic lights and a controller that sleeps. Then the student's own state diagram and the code plan, written in plain English per mode, with the libraries named. Soldering lesson 2 runs at the bench. Biv in progress. Soldering lesson 2: straight pin rows, wire to pad, heat shrink and strain relief. Signed off on a three wire connector that survives a firm pull. The state diagram is the page that answers the AI question, so it is checked individually.
19 The physical half, the paperwork, and the ordering deadline.Spot the bad case drawing and price a real build. Then case drawings front, side and top with a title block and every hole placed and sized, the parts list with catalog links, prices, quantities and delivery times, the requirements page, and the test points. Soldering lesson 3 runs and everyone is signed off before the build starts. Biv Production Pack, six to nine pages, due the day after this class. Hard ordering deadline for anything with a long delivery time. Soldering lesson 3 covers desoldering, fixing a bridge and rescuing a lifted pad. The no solder route earns identical marks, so nobody is required to solder.

Classes 20 to 31 · Criterion C

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
20 A build plan a classmate could follow without asking anything.Try to follow a bad plan, find the critical path, then what could go wrong across five build tasks. Students write the plan with the critical path marked, order deadlines by class number, a finish condition on every row and a backup plan on every critical path row. Then the swap test: a partner reads for five minutes and narrates build class 1 and build class 6, unassisted. Gaps recorded, plan revised. Ci Build Plan, both versions, due the day after this class. Build planner is planned, so this runs on paper. The gap between 5-6 and 7-8 is four specific additions, and they are named on the board. Both versions submitted, since the fixing is the evidence.
21 Build class 1. The whole device on breadboard.Everything wired from the Biv wiring list, nothing permanent. The power check runs before anything is switched on, and it runs before anything is switched on for the rest of the unit. The five minute log stop runs for the first time. Cii, Ciii and Civ all open. Change log written in class, bug diary running. Three routines start today and repeat every build class: reread the specification, power check, five minute log stop. Change log and bug diary with real timestamps are planned, so entries are dated by hand.
22 Build class 2. The code skeleton, modes first.Every mode from the state diagram built as an empty mode that prints its own name and does nothing else. The shape is made right before any behaviour is added. AI is used freely here, and every line is expected to be explainable. Cii, Civ in class. Laptops and boards. A student who cannot map their code back onto their state diagram is caught in this class rather than at code conversation 1.
23 Build class 3. One input and one output working properly.Debouncing so one press does not register as five, converting the raw sensor number into something meaningful, and a real threshold. This is the decision from rule 1 working for the first time, and it goes in the making log as such. Cii, Civ in class. Waiting is a trap runs as a short clinic for anyone whose code freezes while it waits and misses presses. Calibration numbers kept in one place, not scattered.
24 Build class 4. The rest of the inputs and outputs, and the first code conversation.Remaining parts wired and coded. Code conversation 1 runs two minutes per student while everyone else keeps building: three lines pointed at, three questions, what does it do, why is it here, what breaks if it changes. Everything still to be ordered is ordered by the end of today. Cii code conversation 1, Civ in class. Second hard ordering deadline. Students know both conversation classes in advance, so there is no surprise in it.
25 Build class 5. Come off the breadboard.Onto stripboard, connectors or permanent wiring. Layout planned before anything is soldered. Colour coded wire cut to length and anchored so pulling does not break a joint, with at least one connector that unplugs for repairs without being cut. Cii wiring and assembly evidence, Civ in class. Good joint, bad joint and two sets of wiring both run as short clinics. The screw terminal and crimp route earns exactly the same marks as soldering.
26 Build class 6. Make the case.Printed, cut or built from the Biv case drawings. Every hole checked against the real connector, screen and button before anything is glued or closed. The small laser etcher and the 3D printers are reliable, the large laser cutter is not, so any plan depending on it uses its written fallback. Cii controls and case evidence, Civ in class. Printer queue checked at the start of class. A case that does not close is a class lost, so the dry fit happens before glue.
27 Build class 7. Everything goes in the box and runs on its own power.Boards mounted and fixed down, cables tidied, on and off switch fitted. The laptop is unplugged and the device runs by itself for the first time, which is rule 2 tested rather than assumed. Ciii evidence, Cii, Civ in class. Batteries or power supplies confirmed available. Anything still needing a laptop is logged as a change with a plan attached.
28 Build class 8. Labels, finish and the user sheet.Every button and light labelled in words the client can read. Finishing and edges. The one page user sheet written: what it does, how to start it, what each light or sound means, and what to do when it stops working. The label reading check runs at the end of class, from the distance the device is really used at. Ciii presentation evidence, Civ in class. Labels get written 20 cm from the face and read from two metres away, which is why the check is done standing back.
29 Build class 9. Cold start test, then fix what it found.Code conversation 2 runs first, two minutes each. Then devices are swapped and a classmate is handed the device switched on with the user sheet and nothing else. The owner watches in silence and records every wrong guess, every wrong order and every question. The rest of the class is spent fixing what the test found. Cii code conversation 2, Ciii cold start record, Civ in class. The silence is the hard part and it is stated before the test starts. The moment anything is explained, the data is gone.
30 Build class 10. Finish, then make the documents match the device.Last fixes. Making log, bug diary and change log brought up to date. The Biv pack is corrected so it describes the device that actually exists, including the wiring list, the parts list and any Bi rule that named a part that changed. Cii making log with three skills across two areas plus the bug diary, Ciii the finished device with its user sheet and cold start record, and Civ change log due the day after this class for anyone not using the rescue class. Ten build classes should produce well over ten change log rows. An undated log written in one sitting cannot reach the top band.
31 Rescue class. Hard cut off for the whole of Criterion C.Buffer for late parts, failed prints, and devices that still need a laptop or a finger holding a wire. Any student with fewer than three skills in depth across two areas uses this class to close that gap. Students already finished start the Di reading rather than sitting idle. Cii, Ciii and Civ due the day after this class for everyone. No extensions past this point. Added class. A device that cannot run on its own power by the end of today is evaluated as it stands in Dii, and that is said in advance.

Classes 32 to 40 · Criterion D, and close

ClassDaily planAssessment taskNotes and required products
32 Give every rule a test, with instruments where numbers are needed.Match the method, aimed straight at the questionnaire habit, then measure something, taking the current of three circuits with the multimeter wired in properly. Students then work down the Bi page one row at a time, choosing people or instrument and naming what is needed, then checking they have at least three instrument tests and at least two tests with people. Di in progress. Multimeters, stopwatches, and a trusted reference instrument for the accuracy test. Battery life needs a meter, not an opinion.
33 Build the actual test sheets, and run what does not need people.How accurate is that sensor, write five bad questions, and build an observation sheet. Then the real instruments are made: the questionnaire with its questions written out, the observation sheet showing what is counted, the interview questions, the task test naming what is timed and by whom, and the measurement sheet naming the instrument, the units and the trusted value. Any instrument test that does not need people is run today so the client session is free for the ones that do. Di Test Plan with the test sheets attached, due the day after this class. Printing time at the end of class. A test plan with no sheets attached is a list of intentions.
34 Session one with the client. Collect the data.The device goes to the people on the card and they use it. The student runs their own test sheets and collects their own data. The class ends with sheets filled in rather than a general impression, because the write up class has nothing to work from otherwise. Dii data collection. No submission from this class. First of only two client sessions, so the booking is made weeks ahead. The device must run on its own power in the client's room. Bring spare batteries and the user sheet.
35 Judge every rule, then say what the evidence is worth.Passed, partly or failed on shared data first, then how much is this evidence worth, then "it works when I do it". Students write one row per Bi rule with none left blank, then the closing section on the limits: one device, mostly self tested, one session, the wrong room, and testers who wanted to be kind. Dii, every rule judged one by one, due the day after this class. The limits section is worth more than the table above it, and most students write two lines there. Say so before they start. Any rule tested only by the designer is marked as such in the evidence column.
36 Rank improvements by what they actually cost.Name the type, then where is the evidence, where students mark which of their own suggestions have nothing behind them. Then the ranked table with the type labelled as code, wiring, a different part, the case or the whole idea, the Dii evidence behind each one, and the ranking rule stated. Diii, improvements in order of importance, due the day after this class. One improvement must address something that passed. Naming the type is what stops the ranking being a list of what the student would enjoy doing.
37 Session two. The demo, the six questions, and the video.Plan your three minutes first, storyboarded into six shots. Then the device is handed over in the place it would live and the student says nothing for at least ten minutes while the client does the real task. The six impact questions are asked in order. The video is filmed during the session, while the device is being used, not afterwards on a desk. Div evidence gathering. No submission from this class. Second and last client session. Camera or phone, storyboard in hand, consent for filming arranged beforehand. Questions 1, 2 and 4 produce predictions, and they are labelled as predictions at the time.
38 Write the impact report and cut the video.Enjoyed it or changed something, seen it or guessed it, effects nobody intended, and where does it end up. Then the two page report in six sections: who was affected including people who never touch it, intended against actual with the Ai need statement quoted, the evidence from both sessions, unintended effects, at least three wider effects covering repair, disposal, cost and access, and a plain answer on whether the need was met. The three minute video is edited to the storyboard. Div, impact report and demo video, due the day after this class. Editing time and audio from the session needed. Without the video there is no record of the device in use, since students take their devices home at the end of the unit.
39 Make all sixteen uploads complete and consistent.Every ManageBac submission audited against the sixteen strands. The Biv pack checked one last time against the device that exists. Bug diary and change log completed and dated. Anything still missing is either finished in this class or recorded as missing. Final ManageBac audit completed the day after this class. If it is not on ManageBac it cannot be marked, and this class is the last time that is fixable.
40 Slack, handover and the link forward.Held for lost classes anywhere in the unit. If not needed, devices are handed over to clients who want to keep them or taken home, boards are collected back or signed out, and the wider effects section is picked up again as the link forward to the circular economy in DP Design. None. Board return or sign out recorded. Photograph a few finished devices for next year's Aiii black box card examples.

Five points in each unit where being late costs more than one class. If a checkpoint is missed, take the time out of the slack at the end rather than out of the build block, because a shortened build block shows up in Cii, Ciii and Civ at once.
